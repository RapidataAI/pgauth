# Each pgauth process owns its temporary roles, and removal cannot be undone by the role

A temporary role can change its own password and log in to Postgres directly, and can make itself undroppable (a large object, default privileges). So a role is never shared across processes: its name and comment carry the creating process's nonce, whose advisory lock shows the owner is alive. On the last close, and in a sweep every minute for roles whose owner is gone or that outlived any connection, pgauth sets NOLOGIN, revokes the role's groups, terminates every session of it, then drops it; a role that still cannot be dropped stays without login or rights and is logged and counted. Reusing a role resets its password, `CONNECTION LIMIT` bounds its sessions, and `VALID UNTIL` (token expiry plus 5 minutes) bounds how long a self-set password opens new logins (amends 0003: still never the way access ends).
